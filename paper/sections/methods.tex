\section*{How this work was done}\label{sec:methods}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{How this work was done}
\paragraph{Roles.} This paper is the work of its two authors together with current frontier AI models from more than one
developer. The authors set the direction throughout: they chose the questions, fixed the scope and the standards of proof and
review, decided which lines to pursue and which to stop, and take full responsibility for every claim. The models did most of the
exploration, derivation, computation and drafting, in a sequence of short research sprints. For this paper the models first explored exchange rates, recovery schedules and operational access. A subsequent proof audit reconstructed the derivations and checked their scope and cited inputs. Two independent blind audits, by models of another developer that had not taken part in the work, then read the manuscript refutation first, reran the checks and looked up the references in primary records. They found no error in a main result. They asked for a missing hypothesis in Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted}, credit to related work on the cost of erasing correlations, corrected equation and source pointers, and narrower descriptions of the checks; all are applied here.

\paragraph{Validation.} The evidence for the results is of two kinds: complete written proofs and computations by the scripts in
\texttt{verify/}, which the reader can check.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Proofs.} Every theorem is proved here or supplied with an exact result citation. The imported results are the published game-demand laws; the schedule bounds used here are proved directly. The potential lemma accounts for discarded bath records, dephased communication and quantum transfer. The finite-error result states its extraction input explicitly.
\item \emph{Proof audit.} The audit checked the input and reference boundary of the game entropy ledger, the order of limits, the recovery communication budgets, the domain for substituting an extraction-error bound, and the versions of the cited sources. The unrestricted sector stability extension remains open.
\item \emph{Computation.} \texttt{verify/run\_all.py} checks the three erasure penalties, the CHSH family, every magic-square question and outcome, the working-register entropy budget of the swap construction, finite communication tradeoffs, sector allocation against linear programming and dual witnesses, weighted commutator constructions, multiphase clocks, and the two access obstructions. The swap channel itself is not simulated, the transfer-bound checks use pure states, and no finite test evaluates the relative entropy of entanglement of a mixed state. Five negative controls are rejected: a product source assigned a quantum penalty and a wrong magic-square parity, and three sanity assertions (two fixed arithmetic comparisons and the positivity of $h_2(1/3)$) that do not simulate a faulty protocol. Scripts run sequentially with one numerical thread, fixed seeds and small matrices. The recorded logs give the actual environment and resource use. Finite checks support the proofs and do not replace them.
\end{itemize}
No argument here has been formally verified; the proofs and computations are offered for checking as they stand.

\paragraph{Priority.} Bounded searches of primary literature through 27 October 2026 covered work deficit, conditional erasure, the noise cost of erasing correlations, local purity distillation, assisted-erasure exclusivity, Bell-related thermodynamic valuations, and the mathematical tools cited at their use. The comparisons in Section~\ref{sec:access} distinguish these established ingredients from the game-level deductions. The search does not establish exhaustive publication priority.
